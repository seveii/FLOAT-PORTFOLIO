The Computer Engineering side — from embedded systems coursework to the code that actually runs this site.

Ibuild things twice — once so they work, once so they look like they were supposed to look exactly like that. That's the overlap between studying Computer Engineering and moonlighting as a designer: I don't think of "design" and "engineering" as separate skill sets, just two ends of the same process.

Most of what shapes my eye doesn't come from a tutorial. It comes from cafe hopping around Bulacan and Metro Manila with a camera set permanently to black and white, noticing how light falls on a wall or how a menu is laid out. It comes from traveling to places I've been wanting to visit and paying attention to signage, typography, color — the kind of design that isn't trying to be design.

Leading creative direction for ACES as Multimedia Director, and now handling public relations for ICpEP, taught me the part school doesn't: how to take an idea from a group chat to something people actually see and use, on a deadline, with other people relying on it.

When I'm not designing or coding, I'm probably queued up in an online game decompressing — turns out that's where a lot of my patience for debugging comes from.
